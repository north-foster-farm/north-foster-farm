## Spring

{{< figure src="images/brooder.jpg"
  alt="Three-day-old chicks on wood shavings in the brooder, with more gathered under the red glow of a heat lamp along the back wall."
  caption="A brooder of chicks, still only days old." />}}

Spring felt like jumping onto a treadmill that was already moving. We
started in April, at the beginning of our first year raising chickens,
without much idea of what to expect. It wasn't long before we had our
hands full raising the birds, while trying to find the time and energy
for a project list that kept growing. We had a plan for the year, and
we thought the plan ended in the fall. We knew from previous years that
the last market was a finish line, and after it we'd have time to take
a breath.

## Summer

{{< figure src="images/sheep-with-broilers.jpg"
  alt="A sheep lying in the grass beside a wooden chicken pen, with white meat birds inside the wire mesh."
  caption="One of the sheep, keeping the birds company." />}}

We learned a couple of important lessons in the spring, for better or
worse, and went into summer a little more confident. After a few
months, the whirlwind of spring started to feel like managed chaos.
Pasture, processing, and markets. Feed, water, move the birds. Pack the
coolers. Tent goes up, tent comes down. Unpack the coolers. Eggs, eggs,
eggs. While we ran around from sunrise to sunset, the birds watched
from the comfort of their pens, enjoying fresh pasture and the
occasional visit from the sheep. But to us, it felt like a blur. Amid
all of it, one thing stayed constant. We hardly had a chance to feel
discouraged, because so many of our customers kept coming back to the
tent to tell us how dinner turned out. Every one of those conversations
reminded us that what we were doing was worth it.

## Fall

{{< figure src="images/tilted-tent-square.jpg"
  alt="North Foster Farm's market booth on grass: a green tent over a table with a checkered tablecloth, a Chicken & Eggs banner, a whiteboard price list, and a chalkboard sign."
  caption="Our tent at the Tilted Barn this summer." />}}

Fall felt very different. The last week of August was the last week of
the Tilted Barn market, and with it, fall was suddenly close. Back in
the spring, we'd pictured fall as our chance to ease off the gas. Now
that it had arrived, it felt nothing like that. Somewhere between fall
and next spring was a gap we hadn't figured out how to cross.

Whatever the answer was, it had to start with our customers. For most
of them, the tent on Saturday morning was the only place they knew to
find us. With four Saturdays left at Scituate, we had to give them
another way.

By the time our weekly markets wrapped up in Foster on
{{< terms "last-market-day" >}}, the answer was already under way.

## Three quarters of the way

Selling online was an idea from before the business really started, and
it came back the moment we realized our customers would need another
way to reach us. That's when it clicked. We're only three quarters of
the way through our first year.

The farm slows down in winter, but the business doesn't. The last
quarter is the one we know the least about.

## Winter

So here's our answer. We launched online ordering, so you can buy from
us any week of the year without a market to meet at. The order page
keeps up with our stock as it changes, so what you see is what we have.
And since Foster is a long drive in the middle of winter, we now deliver
to your door, priced to be as affordable as we can make it. If you're
not sure whether we deliver to you, or where to pick up, check
[our map](/#map).

We raise our birds the way we think every animal deserves to be raised,
and we hold ourselves to that every day. It's work that matters to us,
and we think it matters to you too. So don't let it stop when the
markets do. We'll be here all winter, and we hope you will be too.
