August Update

{{ partial "home/figure.html" (dict "image" ($lilyImg.Resize "1150x webp q82") "alt" "Lily the sheep looking over the pasture fence on a summer afternoon" "caption" "This is Lily. She doesn’t lay eggs, and she isn’t for sale!" "lazy" false "classes" "mb-4") }}

We can’t believe it’s August already. The summer has gone by so quickly—it feels like we were setting up the brooder a couple of weeks ago, and here we are already past the halfway point of the market season. Things are going great out on the farm.

If we haven’t met yet: we’re North Foster Farm, a small family farm in Foster, Rhode Island. We raise chickens on grass, {{ $moveThemToFreshPastureRegularly }}, and feed them grain that’s {{ $freeOfGMOs }}. We think that’s how chickens should be raised—and we think you can taste the difference.

This week we moved another batch of chicks out of the brooder and onto pasture, which is one of our favorite days of the season. They spend their first few weeks indoors under heat lamps, and once they’re fully feathered they graduate to the field. From then on they live in mobile pens that we move onto fresh grass every day.

We love eggs.

Over the years we’ve purchased all kinds of eggs from the store: inexpensive eggs, eggs called “free-range”, eggs called “cage-free”, and more recently, eggs called “pasture-raised”. Some of these labels sound better than others, but we eventually learned the truth about each of these categories. In fact, pasture-raised eggs stand head and shoulders above the rest.

At North Foster Farm, we proudly produce pasture-raised eggs. At night, our hens are kept inside a secure mobile coop. During the day, they spend their time on grass, enjoying fresh air, sunshine, and abundant access to clean water and fresh feed. They roam freely in a safe enclosure and enjoy grasses and insects—just like chickens are supposed to.

After about a week, when the “good stuff” begins to dwindle, we move them (coop and all) to a new spot in the pasture to start fresh. As a result, we have happy, healthy hens that lay healthy, delicious eggs.
